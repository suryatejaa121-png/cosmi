CosmiFin puts all of it in one mobile app, where every rupee in or out is a workflow with a second person confirming it.

The hardest part

E-signatures with the evidenceIndian courts ask for.

We evaluated Documenso and walked away. Its seal was no stronger than one we could run ourselves, and it would have made a third party part of every court production. So we built signing natively, with OTP-verified signers, a must-scroll reading record, versioned consent and a hash-chained event log.

Each PDF is sealed with a PKCS#7 signature plus an RFC 3161 timestamp, so the evidence outlives the certificate. The PDF signing library had no RFC 3161 timestamp support, so we wrote that signer ourselves on pkijs.

    Electronic signature (email/OTP verified)

    This is not an Aadhaar eSign. It carries no statutory presumption under section 3A of the Information Technology Act, 2000. If this signature is disputed, the party relying on it must prove who signed, using the audit trail below.

    Your PDF reader may show a yellow warning that the signature's validity is "unknown". That means your reader does not recognise CosmiFin's signing certificate — it does not mean the signature is invalid or that the document was altered. Any change to the file after signing would break the seal.

    For a court production

    An electronic record needs a certificate under section 63(4)(c) of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 to be admissible. Part A is generated for you and signed by your officer. Part B must be signed by an independent expert — give them the bundle below and they can verify this record without any access to CosmiFin.

    The certificate is generated fresh each time, so it always names the officer currently in post rather than whoever held the role when this was signed.
